# Casual F1 fans and game design for impact, sharing and return visits (Racing Simplified)

Research date: 2026-10-22. About 20 searches and fetches. Evidence from 2022–2026 unless marked [OLDER]. Several key questions have thin primary evidence, and the Gaps sections say where.

## 1. Who are casual and new F1 fans in 2024–2026?

### Takeaway
New fans are younger and much more often female than the existing base. They arrive through social media, streaming series (Drive to Survive) and shared content, and they attach to drivers and narrative drama first, then stay for "speed, strategy and spectacle". There is no survey data on what confuses them, and nothing measured yet on the effect of the F1 movie on fandom.

### Cited Findings
- The 2025 F1 and Motorsport Network Global Fan Survey had more than 100,000 responses from 186 countries. It runs every four years. The sample is self-selected and highly engaged, so it shows the most attentive fans rather than a representative sample of casual ones. — [Formula1.com](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/formula-1-and-motorsport-network-unveil-2025-global-fan-survey.4YqMebNy8BLaapyJfjzDXO); [Motorsport.com](https://www.motorsport.com/f1/news/formula-1-and-motorsport-network-unveil-2025-global-fan-study/10736345/)
- Women account for three in four new fans, and nearly half of Gen Z respondents are women. — [Formula1.com](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/formula-1-and-motorsport-network-unveil-2025-global-fan-survey.4YqMebNy8BLaapyJfjzDXO); [BlackBook Motorsport](https://www.blackbookmotorsport.com/news/f1-motorsport-network-study-women-gen-z-fans-sponsors-july-2025/)
- Female fans are 25% of surveyed respondents, more than double the 2017 share. Another outlet gives "42% of the global audience". These are different metrics and should not be mixed. — [Survey microsite](https://fansurvey2025-formula1.motorsportnetwork.com/); [Newsworthy](https://www.newsworthy.org.au/women-in-formula-one-academy-2676880553.html)
- 61% engage with F1 content daily. In the US, 70% of Gen Z respondents do. 90% say they are emotionally invested in race outcomes, and two thirds feel personally inspired by drivers or teams. — [Formula1.com](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/formula-1-and-motorsport-network-unveil-2025-global-fan-survey.4YqMebNy8BLaapyJfjzDXO)
- What fans value: speed and performance are the strongest motivators. Driver personalities and narrative drama drive identity-based connection. Fans are drawn in by the breadth of stories and stay for "speed, strategy, and spectacle". In the US, 40% follow a driver first rather than a team or the sport. — [Formula1.com](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/formula-1-and-motorsport-network-unveil-2025-global-fan-survey.4YqMebNy8BLaapyJfjzDXO)
- Entry points among all respondents: TV coverage 24%, social media 12%, driver interest 12%, Drive to Survive 10%. Newer and younger fans skew towards social media and streaming. — summarised from the survey via search results ([survey microsite](https://fansurvey2025-formula1.motorsportnetwork.com/)). The exact percentages were not checked against the PDF, which was too large to fetch.
- [OLDER, 2022] Nielsen found that Drive to Survive brought in more than 360,000 new US F1 fans. 46% of them were 34 or younger, against 16% among non-docuseries F1 viewers. They were more Hispanic (23% vs 12%) and more affluent. 41% of Drive to Survive viewers then watched the first three weekends of the 2022 season. — [Nielsen](https://www.nielsen.com/insights/2022/driven-to-watch-how-a-sports-docuseries-drove-u-s-fans-to-formula-1/)
- [OLDER, 2022] Morning Consult: 74% of US fans under 45 credit their fandom at least partly to Drive to Survive. US F1 fandom is up 33% since 2020. — [Morning Consult](https://morningconsult.com/2022/03/15/f1-fandom-netflix)
- [2023] Opinium (US): 81% of Drive to Survive viewers found the show easy to understand, against 19% who found it difficult. This measures the show's clarity, not fans' grasp of the rules. — [Opinium report](https://opinium.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/F1-Thought-Leadership-Report-US.pdf), via search snippet only; the PDF could not be parsed.
- [2022] A Ferrari fan poll with 50,000+ respondents reported confusion over rules that are "too complicated", with no percentage given. — [Auto123](https://www.auto123.com/en/news/amp/f1-ferrari-survey-shows-fans-unhappy-with-new-formula-1/36023/)
- The F1 movie (released 25 June 2025) took about $144m worldwide on its opening weekend. Motorsport Magazine says it drew more viewers in that weekend than the latest Drive to Survive series did in its first two weeks; the article is inconsistent on that window. — [Motorsport Magazine](https://www.motorsportmagazine.com/articles/racing-movies/f1-movie-outguns-drive-to-survive-in-144m-opening-weekend/)
- Scepticism: Jacques Villeneuve said the film "can bring a lot of fans, but that doesn't make them race fans". — [PlanetF1](https://www.planetf1.com/news/brad-pitt-f1-film-may-bring-fans-for-not-right-reasons-jacques-villeneuve)
- Some women report stigma for having found F1 through Drive to Survive. — [Newsworthy](https://www.newsworthy.org.au/women-in-formula-one-academy-2676880553.html)
- F1 Fantasy in 2024: 25% of registered players were female and 77% were under 35. In 2025 more than 2.75 million teams were entered, a record; that counts teams, not players. — [GP247](https://www.grandprix247.com/formula-1-news/join-the-gp247-f1-fantasy-league-this-season); [FanAmp](https://www.fanamp.com/f1-news/mini-leagues-thrive-in-another-successful-season-for-f1-fantasy-2025)

### Inferences
- The core persona is young, often female, phone-first and daily-engaged. They enter through characters and drama, and they are emotionally invested in outcomes. Activities should start from a driver or rivalry story and reveal strategy as the reason the story happened, not lead with technical concepts.
- Because 40% of US fans follow a driver first, a "pick your driver" entry point (personalising the whole activity) fits how this audience attaches to the sport.
- Rules confusion is widely assumed and anecdotally reported, but it is unmeasured. It is reasonable to teach tyres, the undercut, DRS and penalties only inside the moment that matters for the narrative.
- Fantasy demographics show that young and female casual fans will play structured prediction games. Fantasy is weekly and long-term, though, so a post-race site can differentiate with one-sitting experiences.

### Gaps
- No quantitative source found on which specific concepts confuse new fans (tyres, penalties, strategy).
- No published measurement of the F1 movie's effect on fandom: post-release survey, demographics or retention.
- No 2024–2026 Nielsen report specific to F1 was found.
- The 2025 survey PDF could not be fetched (over 10MB), so its finer breakdowns (motivation rankings, preferred content formats) are unverified.

## 2. Which game mechanics create the "epic" feeling and lasting impact in short web experiences?

### Takeaway
The best-documented engines are these: a shared, limited, curated daily challenge (Wordle, Connections, Immaculate Grid); comparison with the crowd through rarity or percentage scores; and personal identity reveals (the NYT dialect quiz, Spotify Wrapped). There are no controlled studies of "epic feeling" in news games. The evidence is case-based.

### Cited Findings
- Wordle's single shared daily puzzle built community, because everyone solves the same word. Wardle said a random word each day would not have caught on in the same way. — [CUNY Pressbooks, The Wordle Effect](https://pressbooks.cuny.edu/inspire/part/the-wordle-effect-how-five-letters-changed-the-internet/); [BuzzFeed News](https://www.buzzfeednews.com/article/stefficao/how-wordle-went-viral-strategy) [2022]
- NYT Connections was the Times' most successful internal games launch since Spelling Bee. Its head of games credited human curation and the feeling of competing each day against the puzzle's constructor. The Times does not publish player counts. — [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90938716/The%20quest%20to%20create%20the%20next%20Wordle) [2023]
- Immaculate Grid (launched April 2023) is a 3x3 grid with nine guesses and one shot per day ("one false answer dooms your chance at a perfect grid"). It reached tens of thousands of daily players and spun off NFL, NBA and NHL versions. Its rarity score rates each pick by how many others chose it, lower being better, which turns an easy question into a status game. — [Baseball-Reference Bullpen](https://www.baseball-reference.com/bullpen/Immaculate_Grid); [InsideHook](https://www.insidehook.com/sports/immaculate-grid-obsession/amp); [JHU Science of Learning](https://scienceoflearning.jhu.edu/tof/beyond-perfection-the-allure-of-the-immaculate-grid)
- Criticism: one writer says the rarity score provides "no real value aside from internet bragging rights". Bragging rights are exactly the social payoff. — [The Inside Edge](https://theinsideedge.substack.com/p/immaculate-upgrade)
- [OLDER, 2013] The NYT dialect quiz, which outputs a personal map of "where you're from", was the most-viewed page in the paper's history and its most popular content of 2013. — [Knight Lab](https://knightlab.northwestern.edu/tag/canvas/); [WNYC](https://wnyc.org/story/the-man-behind-the-dialect-quiz)
- Spotify Wrapped 2025 added Listening Age, Clubs (six listening-style groups) and Wrapped Party, a competitive mode played with friends. These are identity labels plus social comparison. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/04/spotify-says-wrapped-2025-is-its-biggest-yet-with-200m-users-in-its-first-day)
- Wrapped's "top X% of listeners" is described as a shareable status marker. The claim that Spotify aimed for "moments of surprise and social currency" comes from a secondary blog citing a 2021 Digiday interview and is unverified. — [Markhub24](https://www.markhub24.com/post/spotify-wrapped-turning-data-into-a-global-marketing-event)
- Wrapped 2024's AI-heavy design drew backlash. In 2025 Spotify restored fan favourites (top genres, total minutes), and the result was a record. — [Spartan Shield](https://spartanshield.org/50333/feature/spotify-wrapped-2025-generates-mixed-reactions-amid-ongoing-platform-frustrations/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/04/spotify-says-wrapped-2025-is-its-biggest-yet-with-200m-users-in-its-first-day)
- News gamification research (2022–2026) is mostly qualitative: content analyses, interviews with media leaders and theoretical models. A 2024 analysis found news games improve understanding of complex topics and evoke emotional responses, but did not measure audience outcomes. — [Univ. of Vaasa, Playful approaches to news engagement](https://osuva.uwasa.fi/items/58b1a3fc-7e9f-4580-9096-001f993cc519/full); [LSE Polis, The gamification of journalism](https://blogs.lse.ac.uk/polis/2022/02/24/the-gamification-of-journalism); [KNU, Play the News and Level up](https://cimc.knu.ua/en/article/view/3036)
- One programming-education meta-analysis found the gamification benefit (d = 0.4) was driven mainly by levels, not points or badges. — [ISCTE repository](https://repositorio.iscte-iul.pt/handle/10071/29213?locale=en)

### Inferences
- The "epic" feeling most likely comes from: (a) real stakes framed as a decision (box now or stay out?), (b) a reveal that compares your choice with what actually happened and with what other players chose ("62% boxed; Verstappen didn't"), and (c) an identity verdict at the end ("You are a Lambiase-grade strategist"). Each element appears in at least one proven product: Immaculate Grid rarity, Wrapped and the dialect quiz.
- Scarcity and a shared event matter. One activity per race weekend works as a natural "daily puzzle" cadence, since the whole audience plays the same race. This mirrors Wordle's argument for a single shared puzzle.
- Ranks and levels (strategist tiers) look more useful than points alone, by analogy with the levels finding. This is an inference from education, not tested on news games.
- Streaks need frequency. With about 24 races a season, a "weekend streak" (races played in a row) is plausible, but there is no evidence for streaks at weekly cadence.

### Gaps
- No postmortem from the NYT Games team with numbers on retention mechanics (streaks, archive) was found. The Times does not publish player counts.
- No study isolates "epic feeling" or role-play (strategist or steward) in short web experiences.
- No data was found on percentage-of-players reveals ("X% chose this") affecting retention, beyond the anecdotal appeal of Immaculate Grid.

## 3. What makes a result shareable, and which formats travel on WhatsApp, Instagram Stories and X?

### Takeaway
Shareable results are spoiler-free, identity-signalling and comparable. The Wordle grid shows a result without giving away the answer; Wrapped turns data into a statement about who you are. Most sharing happens privately (messaging, "dark social"), so a strong link preview and a native-sized image matter more than public posts. Format-specific (9:16, OG preview) evidence was not found.

### Cited Findings
- The Wordle emoji grid came from players, not the designer. Early New Zealand players used coloured squares to share results without spoilers, and Wardle then built this into the share button. — [BuzzFeed News](https://www.buzzfeednews.com/article/stefficao/how-wordle-went-viral-strategy) [2022]
- Wordle grew from 90 players (1 Nov 2021) to about 300,000 (2 Jan 2022) and 2 million the following week, with 1.2 million grids shared on Twitter that week. Another source claims 10 million by January 2022, which conflicts and is less reliable. — [CUNY Pressbooks](https://pressbooks.cuny.edu/inspire/part/the-wordle-effect-how-five-letters-changed-the-internet/)
- Unexplained grids in feeds created curiosity ("what do these squares mean?") and a sense of belonging to a club. — [Westminster World](https://westminsterworld.shorthandstories.com/what-is-wordle-and-why-is-it-popular-all-of-a-sudden/index.html); [BuzzFeed News](https://www.buzzfeednews.com/article/stefficao/how-wordle-went-viral-strategy)
- Wordle brought "tens of millions" of new users to the NYT. — [AOL/Yahoo](https://www.aol.com/news/wordle-brought-tens-millions-users-150000203.html)
- Spotify Wrapped 2025: more than 200 million engaged users in the first 24 hours (2024 took 62 hours to reach that) and more than 500 million shares, up 41% year on year. Shares include native shares, screenshots and downloads. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/04/spotify-says-wrapped-2025-is-its-biggest-yet-with-200m-users-in-its-first-day); [Music Week](https://www.musicweek.com/digital/read/spotify-wrapped-2025-was-biggest-ever-with-200-million-engaged-users/093178)
- Wrapped is distributed through Instagram Stories, Facebook, X, Snapchat and image downloads. — [Markhub24](https://www.markhub24.com/post/spotify-wrapped-turning-data-into-a-global-marketing-event) (secondary)
- [OLDER, 2016–2019] Dark social: 63% of people use private messaging to share content (GlobalWebIndex and We Are Social, UK and US). Among messaging apps: Messenger 82%, WhatsApp 56%, Instagram DM 34%. A vendor (RadiumOne) claimed 97% of Six Nations rugby sharing and 72% of Olympic sharing went through dark social. These are vendor claims. — [Research Live](https://www.research-live.com/article/news/most-content-shared-on-private-messaging-apps/id/5051652); [The Drum](https://www.thedrum.com/opinion/2018/03/16/why-marketers-targeting-sports-fans-should-try-their-luck-with-dark-social)

### Inferences
- An F1 result card should work like the Wordle grid. It encodes the player's decisions against reality (for example a row of lap or pit-call blocks: green for matching the winning call, amber for a defensible alternative, red for a disaster) without spoiling the race outcome for friends who haven't watched.
- Ship two artefacts: (1) a text or emoji block that pastes into WhatsApp and X (the Wordle pattern, which works in group chats); (2) a vertical image card for Instagram Stories with an identity label and a percentile ("better than 81% of players"), the Wrapped pattern.
- Because sharing is mostly private, each activity needs a per-result URL with a custom Open Graph image, so the WhatsApp preview carries the hook. This follows from the dark-social findings; it was not directly measured.
- Comparison drives replies ("I'd have boxed too"). Showing friends' or crowd percentages invites a challenge loop: "can you beat my call?"

### Gaps
- No 2022–2026 data was found on which share formats (9:16 image, emoji text, link preview) perform best on WhatsApp, Instagram Stories or X.
- Spotify's own newsroom post on 2025 Wrapped was not read directly; the figures come via TechCrunch and Music Week.

## 4. Session-length and mobile UX norms for interactive news and sports pieces

### Takeaway
I found no credible, news-specific benchmark for ideal duration or completion and drop-off rates of interactive pieces on mobile. The best general evidence says attention falls sharply after the first screenfuls, so the hook and first decision must come immediately.

### Cited Findings
- [OLDER, 2018] Nielsen Norman Group eye-tracking: 57% of page-viewing time was above the fold and 74% in the first two screenfuls (up to 2160px). Attention erodes sharply after the fold, the same pattern as in 2010. Users do scroll, but attention decays. — [NN/g, Scrolling and attention](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolling-and-attention/)
- 2026 arXiv between-subjects study (privacy policies, not news): scrollytelling matched other formats on comprehension, raised engagement and enjoyment, lowered cognitive load against text, and was competitive on completion time. A heavier interactive visualisation was slower. — [arXiv 2603.04367](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.04367)
- A 24-person health-communication pilot: scrollytelling had fewer comprehension errors (not statistically significant) and better experience ratings. — via search summary ([showreel.mdh.ustp.at](https://showreel.mdh.ustp.at/?p=9230)); low weight
- Vendor claims that scrollytelling gets "3–5x time on page" have no methodology and should not be cited as evidence. — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/marketingwithinzuoo/scrollytelling-is-quietly-changing-how-people-read-watch-and-buy-online-and-most-brands-still-1j76)
- Practitioner advice: measure scroll depth and section-level completion rather than bounce, since a "bounce" can mean the user finished the whole piece. — [Maglr](https://www.maglr.com/blog/scrollytelling-what-is-it-and-examples-of-how-to-make-it-work-for-your-brand)

### Inferences
- Design for a first decision inside the first screen (no intro wall), and keep the core loop short. Daily-puzzle peers (Wordle, Connections, Immaculate Grid) are all completed in a few minutes. That is an observation about the genre; it was not measured in these sources.
- Heavier interactive visualisations cost time against simpler formats (the arXiv study). Prefer large tap targets and binary or ternary choices over drag-heavy charts on phones.
- Instrument per-step completion so the real drop-off curve of Racing Simplified becomes the benchmark.

### Gaps
- No public completion or drop-off rates for NYT, Guardian, BBC or other publishers' interactive news games or sports interactives were found.
- No NN/g mobile-specific study with 2022–2026 data was found in this pass.
- No evidence-based "ideal duration" for one-sitting interactive sports pieces.

## 5. Do learning-by-playing approaches work for explaining tyres, undercut, DRS and penalties? Is there evidence?

### Takeaway
Yes, with good general evidence and no F1-specific evidence. Asking people to predict or decide before showing the answer (pretesting and retrieval practice), plus immediate feedback, reliably improves learning. Gamification shows positive but very heterogeneous effects in education.

### Cited Findings
- Li, Ma & Shi (2023) meta-analysis: 41 studies, 49 samples, 5,000+ participants. Gamification had a large overall effect on learning outcomes (g = 0.822, 95% CI 0.567–1.078), moderated by discipline, design principles and environment. — [Frontiers in Psychology](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1253549/full)
- A 2026 higher-education meta-analysis reported d of about 1.12 from 22 studies, but heterogeneity was extreme (I² of about 98.8%), so the estimate is fragile. — [SAGE Open](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/21582440261421375)
- [OLDER, 2017] Adesope et al. meta-analysis: practice testing beats restudying (g of about 0.61) across 272 effect sizes, about 15,000 participants. The effect is robust across delays and similar in lab and classroom. Whether feedback adds benefit is reported inconsistently across summaries. — [Learning Scientists](https://www.learningscientists.org/blog/2017/2/9-1); [WSU PDF](https://education.wsu.edu/documents/2018/01/rethinking-use-tests.pdf/)
- Pretesting effect: guessing before learning improves later memory, even when the guesses are wrong. Proposed mechanisms are prediction-error learning and stronger processing of the corrective feedback that follows. Learners underestimate this benefit and believe reading works better. — [Pan & Rivers 2023, PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9839203/); [PMC 2025, pretesting and feedback timing](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12292081/)
- 2025 work: pretesting's distinct advantage is in how new information is encoded, while retrieval practice consolidates what is already learned. — [PMC 2025](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12292081/)
- News-game learning evidence is weak: self-reports, interviews and content analyses, with no controlled pre- and post-tests. — [Univ. of Vaasa](https://osuva.uwasa.fi/items/58b1a3fc-7e9f-4580-9096-001f993cc519/full); [AEJMC TJMC, Colley 2022](https://aejmc.us/wp-content/uploads/sites/9/2022/07/TJMC-12.1-Colley-.pdf)

### Inferences
- The "decide first, then reveal what really happened and why" loop maps directly onto the pretesting and prediction-error mechanism. Asking "Would you undercut on lap 18?" before explaining the undercut should teach it better than an explainer card. A wrong call followed by a clear reveal is a feature, not a failure.
- Feedback should come right after each decision and be concrete, for example "Norris pitted, gained 2.1s on fresh tyres, came out ahead". It should tie the concept to the race moment, not to a glossary.
- Learners don't notice they are learning (Pan & Rivers), so the activity should not be framed as a lesson. Frame it as play and let the understanding arrive as a side effect, which suits a casual audience.
- Effect sizes come from education settings with repeated exposure. A single 3–5 minute post-race play will produce smaller, less durable gains, and repeat weekends act as spaced practice.

### Gaps
- No study found on learning motorsport (or any sport's) rules through play.
- No measured evidence that news games improve knowledge in controlled conditions.
